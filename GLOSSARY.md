# The Running Glossary

A working glossary of running: 129 terms, from your first easy run to a 100-miler, in plain language. Written from an Indian runner's point of view; the terms themselves are global.

## Training runs

- **Easy run** - A relaxed run at a pace where you can hold a conversation. Most of your weekly running should live here.
- **Long run** - The longest run of your week, usually done easy. Builds endurance and teaches you to keep going.
- **Recovery run** - A short, very gentle run the day after a hard session. Loosens the legs more than it trains them.
- **Tempo run** - A sustained effort at "comfortably hard", roughly the pace you could hold for an hour. Builds the strength to run fast without blowing up.
- **Intervals** - Repeated fast segments with jog or walk breaks between them, like 6 x 800 m. The classic way to build speed.
- **Fartlek** - Swedish for "speed play". Unstructured faster bits mixed into a run: hard to the lamppost, easy to the tree.
- **Strides** - Short accelerations of 15 to 30 seconds, usually at the end of an easy run. They wake the legs up without tiring them.
- **Hill repeats** - Hard runs up a slope with easy jogs back down. Strength work disguised as running.
- **Progression run** - A run that starts easy and finishes fast. Teaches you to close well.
- **Base building** - Weeks of mostly easy mileage before the serious training starts. The foundation everything else sits on.
- **Taper** - Cutting training back in the final one to three weeks before a race so you arrive fresh.
- **Peak week** - The biggest training week of a plan, usually two to four weeks before race day.
- **Doubles** - Two runs in one day. Splits the load for runners chasing higher mileage.
- **Cross-training** - Aerobic work that is not running: cycling, swimming, the elliptical. Fitness without the pounding.
- **Warmup** - Ten to fifteen minutes of easy jogging and drills before fast running. Cold legs run badly and get hurt.
- **Cooldown** - Easy jogging or walking after a hard effort to bring the body down gently.
- **Brick** - Back-to-back sessions of two sports, usually bike then run. A triathlon staple.

## Pacing and effort

- **Pace** - How fast you are running, in minutes per kilometre. Lower is faster.
- **Split** - Your time for one segment of a run or race, usually each kilometre.
- **Negative split** - Running the second half faster than the first. The way good races are usually run.
- **Positive split** - Slowing down in the second half. Usually a sign you went out too fast.
- **Even splits** - Holding the same pace from start to finish. Harder than it sounds.
- **PB (personal best)** - Your fastest ever time over a distance. Also called a PR (personal record).
- **SB (season's best)** - Your fastest time in the current season.
- **RPE (rate of perceived exertion)** - How hard a run feels on a scale of 1 to 10. No watch needed.
- **Talk test** - A simple effort check. Full sentences mean easy running; single words mean you are working hard.
- **Heart rate zones** - Effort bands based on your heart rate, from very easy Zone 1 to all-out Zone 5. Most training belongs in the low zones.
- **Zone 2** - The easy aerobic zone where conversation still flows. Builds the engine and forms the bulk of most training plans.
- **Threshold pace** - The fastest pace you can sustain for about an hour, sitting just below the point where fatigue spirals. A key fitness marker.
- **Race pace** - The pace you are targeting on race day. Training touches it often so it feels familiar.
- **Bonk (hitting the wall)** - Running out of fuel mid-run. Sudden, heavy and awful. In a marathon it often shows up around 30 to 35 km if you have not eaten enough.
- **Kick** - The finishing sprint at the end of a race.

## Racing and race day

Standard race distances:

| Race | Distance |
| --- | --- |
| 5K | 5 km |
| 10K | 10 km |
| Half marathon | 21.0975 km |
| Marathon | 42.195 km |
| Ultra | Anything longer than a marathon |

- **Bib** - Your race number, pinned to your chest. Often carries the timing chip.
- **Timing chip** - The small tag that records when you cross the start and finish mats.
- **Gun time** - Your time measured from the starting gun. Prize results often use it.
- **Chip time (net time)** - Your time from when you personally cross the start mat to the finish. What you actually ran.
- **Corral** - A grouped start area based on expected pace, so faster runners go first and nobody trips over anyone.
- **Wave start** - Sending the field off in batches a few minutes apart to thin out the course.
- **Flag-off** - The formal start of the race. Indian races love a flag-off, often by a chief guest with a horn.
- **Expo** - The event before race day where you collect your bib and wander stalls selling gear and gels.
- **Bib collection (packet pickup)** - Picking up your bib and race kit before race day. Many Indian races require it in person the day before.
- **Aid station** - A table on the course handing out water, electrolytes and sometimes food.
- **Course marshal** - A volunteer stationed on the route to point runners the right way.
- **Cut-off** - The latest time allowed to finish, or to reach a checkpoint. Miss it and you are out of the official race.
- **Sweeper** - The vehicle or runner that follows the last participant and closes the course behind them.
- **Pacer** - An official runner who holds a steady speed so others can ride along to a target finish time. Often carries a flag or balloon with the time on it.
- **DNF (did not finish)** - You started but did not complete the race. It happens to everyone eventually.
- **DNS (did not start)** - You registered but never made the start line.
- **DQ (disqualified)** - Removed from the results, usually for cutting the course or taking outside help.
- **Age category** - Results grouped by age bands, so a 50-year-old races other 50-somethings rather than 22-year-olds.
- **Veteran (masters) category** - Age-group racing for older runners, usually from 40 or 45 up. Many Indian races award separate veteran prizes.
- **Open category** - The main results bracket everyone is ranked in before age categories are applied.
- **BQ (Boston qualifier)** - A marathon time fast enough to apply for the Boston Marathon. A bucket-list goal for many marathoners.
- **Certified course** - A route officially measured to be the exact advertised distance. Only these count for records and qualifying times.
- **AIMS-certified** - A course measured to the international AIMS standard. Most big Indian city marathons are, which is why their times count for qualification elsewhere.
- **Timing certificate** - The official result document from a race, used as proof of your time when other races ask for a qualifier.
- **Point-to-point** - A course that finishes somewhere different from where it starts.
- **Loop course** - A course that returns to its start. Can be one big loop or several laps.
- **Out-and-back** - Run out along a route, turn around, come back the same way.
- **Finisher medal** - The medal everyone gets for completing the race. Not for winning, for finishing.
- **Goal race** - The race your whole training block points at.

## Gear and shoes

- **Heel-to-toe drop** - The height difference between a shoe's heel and forefoot, in millimetres. Zero drop mimics barefoot; a high drop softens heel striking.
- **Stack height** - How much sole sits between your foot and the ground. More stack, more cushioning.
- **Super shoe** - A racing shoe with a stiff carbon plate and springy foam. Real speed gains, real price tag, and the foam wears out fast.
- **Racing flats** - Light, thin racing shoes. The old-school fast option before super shoes took over.
- **Trainers** - Your everyday running shoes: durable and comfortable rather than featherlight.
- **Trail shoes** - Shoes with deeper grip and more protection for dirt, mud and rock.
- **Lugs** - The rubber teeth on a trail shoe's outsole. Bigger lugs bite into mud better.
- **Pronation** - The natural inward roll of the foot as it lands. Too much of it is called overpronation.
- **Neutral shoe** - A shoe without extra support features, for runners whose feet roll normally.
- **Stability shoe** - A shoe with firmer foam on the inner side to calm overpronation.
- **Toe box** - The front of the shoe where your toes live. A roomy one saves toenails on long runs.
- **Upper** - Everything above the sole. Modern uppers are thin, breathable engineered mesh.
- **Last** - The foot-shaped form a shoe is built around. It decides the shoe's basic fit.
- **Hydration vest** - A snug running backpack with bottles or a bladder. Standard kit for trails and ultras.
- **Running belt** - A waist belt that carries a phone, keys and gels without bouncing.
- **Energy gel** - A small packet of fast carbohydrate for fuelling mid-run. Best taken with water.
- **Anti-chafe balm** - Lubricant applied where skin or fabric rubs. Prevents the long-run chafe you will never forget.
- **GPS watch** - A watch that tracks distance, pace and usually heart rate. The modern runner's dashboard.
- **Heart rate strap** - A chest strap that reads heart rate more accurately than a watch's optical sensor.
- **Footpod** - A small shoe sensor that measures pace and cadence where GPS struggles, like on treadmills.

## How the body works

- **VO2 max** - The maximum oxygen your body can use during hard exercise. A headline measure of aerobic fitness.
- **Lactate threshold** - The intensity where lactate builds faster than the body can clear it. Above it, you are on borrowed time.
- **Aerobic** - Effort powered by oxygen: easy to steady running you can sustain for a long time.
- **Anaerobic** - Effort so hard that oxygen alone cannot keep up, like a sprint. Short, sharp and it burns.
- **Running economy** - How much energy you spend at a given pace. Better economy means the same speed costs you less.
- **Cadence** - Steps per minute. Many runners land near 170 to 180; overstriders usually run lower.
- **Stride length** - The distance covered with each step. It grows naturally with speed; forcing it causes overstriding.
- **Overstriding** - Landing with your foot far ahead of your body. A braking move that stresses shins and knees.
- **Ground contact time** - How long each foot spends on the ground per step. Less time generally means faster running.
- **HRV (heart rate variability)** - The tiny variation between heartbeats. Many runners track it as a rough signal of recovery and readiness.
- **Glycogen** - Carbohydrate stored in your muscles and liver. Your main fuel for running hard; when it runs out, you bonk.
- **Carb loading** - Eating extra carbohydrate in the days before a long race to top up glycogen stores.
- **Electrolytes** - Salts like sodium and potassium that you lose in sweat. Worth replacing on long or hot runs.
- **Sweat rate** - How much fluid you lose per hour of running. Knowing yours shapes your drinking plan, especially in Indian summers.
- **DOMS (delayed onset muscle soreness)** - The stiffness that peaks a day or two after a hard session. Normal, and it passes.
- **Supercompensation** - The rebound after training stress: recover well and the body comes back slightly stronger.
- **Overtraining** - Too much stress with too little recovery. Shows up as heavy legs, poor sleep and stalled times, and takes weeks to undo.
- **Periodization** - Organising training into blocks such as base, build, peak and taper so fitness arrives on race day.

## Trail and ultra

- **Ultra (ultramarathon)** - Any race longer than a marathon. Common distances are 50K, 50 miles, 100K and 100 miles.
- **50K** - The entry-level ultra, about 8 km beyond a marathon.
- **100-miler** - The classic ultra distance. Usually involves running through the night.
- **Elevation gain (vert)** - Total metres climbed over a route. Trail races advertise it proudly, and 2,000 m of gain changes everything.
- **D+** - Short for dénivelé positif, French for total climbing. You will see it in European trail race descriptions.
- **Singletrack** - A narrow trail, one person wide. The good stuff.
- **Technical terrain** - Ground full of rocks, roots and steep bits that demands attention with every step.
- **Switchback** - A sharp zigzag turn on a steep climb or descent.
- **FKT (fastest known time)** - The quickest recorded run of a route, usually an unofficial trail challenge.
- **Drop bag** - A bag you hand to organisers before the start, delivered to a mid-course checkpoint. Holds spare socks, food and hope.
- **Crew** - Friends or family allowed to meet you at set checkpoints during an ultra with food, gear and encouragement.
- **Trail pacer** - In longer ultras, a companion allowed to run the final stretch with you for safety and company.
- **Mandatory gear** - Kit the race requires you to carry, like a jacket, whistle or headlamp. It gets checked, and you can be DQ'd without it.
- **Gaiters** - Fabric covers strapped over your shoes to keep stones and sand out.
- **Trekking poles** - Lightweight poles used on steep mountain races. Common in the Alps, rarer elsewhere.
- **Headlamp** - A torch strapped to your forehead. Essential for night sections in ultras and pre-dawn trail starts.

## Injuries and niggles

- **Niggle** - A small ache that is not an injury yet. Every runner's early warning light.
- **Runner's knee** - Pain around or behind the kneecap, flaring on stairs and downhills. One of the most common running injuries.
- **Shin splints** - Aching along the inner shin, usually from ramping up training too quickly.
- **IT band syndrome** - Sharp pain on the outer knee from an irritated iliotibial band. Loves long downhills.
- **Plantar fasciitis** - Stabbing heel pain, worst with the first steps of the morning. A stubborn one.
- **Achilles tendinitis** - Pain and stiffness in the Achilles tendon. Back off early or it lingers.
- **Stress fracture** - A tiny bone crack from repeated impact. The one running injury that always means stopping.
- **Side stitch** - A sharp pain under the ribs mid-run. Slow down, breathe deeply, press on the spot.
- **Black toenail** - Bruising under the nail from toes hitting the front of the shoe. A marathoner's badge; the nail often retires.
- **Chafing** - Skin rubbed raw by fabric or skin. Salt and sweat make it worse.
- **Blister** - A fluid pocket from friction. Good socks, fitted shoes and lubricant keep them rare.
- **Runner's high** - The wave of calm euphoria that shows up on a good long run. Real, and worth the entry fee.
- **RICE (rest, ice, compression, elevation)** - The classic first response to a fresh soft-tissue injury.
- **Foam rolling** - Self-massage with a hard foam cylinder. Hurts kindly and loosens tight spots.
